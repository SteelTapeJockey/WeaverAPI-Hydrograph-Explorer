Period shown — drag the ends

Over the Top method. Each well gets one value per year, is checked for spikes, and must have enough years of record. Its values are then standardized (or turned into year-to-year changes), and every year with enough wells gets the median across wells, with a 95% bootstrap band. A well counts in every year it was measured: no schedule, no bins, nothing shifted between years.

Years with fewer wells than the minimum are left empty (red bars under the chart); years with fewer than 10 wells are drawn with hollow markers. The trend and per-well slopes are Theil–Sen with a Mann–Kendall test. For hydrograph clusters, use Splitting the data → Group by → Hydrograph clusters. Starting values follow the literature (Jasechko et al. 2024; Bloomfield & Marchant 2013; Bloomfield et al. 2015) and are not yet validated for New Mexico.

Group by…

Splits the qualifying wells into groups and draws a mean and median for each, so unlike wells aren't averaged together. Split by depth (cut points chosen from the selected wells, or typed) or by a category such as formation, USGS aquifer, aquifer extent, study area or data source (the largest groups are drawn and the rest combined). "Depth to water" uses each well's average depth to water in the current data; "well depth" is the drilled depth (wells without it are left out).

Season

Restricts every well's readings to a month range before anything else is computed — winter and summer water levels aren't the same, especially near irrigation. A range that crosses the new year counts as one season: Winter (Nov–Feb) 2020 is Nov–Dec 2019 plus Jan–Feb 2020, the same definition as the kriged averages and NMBGMR OFR-616.

What this tool does

Builds composite hydrographs for New Mexico wells. Each well is reduced to one value per year and standardized, and every year with enough wells gets the median across wells with a bootstrap band (the Over the Top method), so wells with different depths and monitoring histories can be combined on one chart. Data are pulled live from the NM Unified Water Data API (NMBGMR, USGS, ST2 agencies, ISC and others).

Quick start

  1. Pick a study area, aquifer, extent, agency or county in the filters, or draw/select wells on the map.
  2. Pick the composite method (normal score, z-score or chain-linked feet) and the minimum wells per year and record length.
  3. Press Build composite. Click the chart to highlight a year; click a well to see its own hydrograph.
  4. Change the normalization (feet, USGS-style, elevation) or season to compare behavior.

Abilities

  • Group and colour by aquifer, extent, formation, agency or live API layers (trends, status, water quality).
  • Wells measured by several agencies are linked and ringed; keep one per cluster if you wish.
  • Overview mode plots every selected well; single-well export to CSV, GeoJSON and PNG.
  • Reading quality filters (static levels only, exclude pumping/affected, approved only, logger vs manual) recompute every average and trend; well statistics and water quality filters use the API's trend, sampling, recency, MCL, water-type, hardness and TDS tables; the single-well view can plot a chemistry series.
  • Download the raw readings for selected wells (original schema) as a zip.
  • Export the composite as PNG with legend, or as CSV.

Good to know

  • The first visit downloads every water-level reading (about 880,000) and saves it in this browser, so later visits take seconds. Shift+click the live-data chip in the header to refresh everything.
  • Averages are simple means of readings per month, then per year; depth to water is in feet below ground. Possible data spikes are flagged and can be excluded.
  • A year is drawn only when at least the minimum number of wells (default 5) were measured in it, so very small selections may show nothing.
  • Well points appear once you zoom in to about county scale or pick an area, and the composite is built once something is selected; this keeps the first view fast.
Dots are the mean of all readings in each month (the dataset stores monthly means, not individual readings); the orange line is the yearly value, chosen above. Flagged months (red ×) are left out of the yearly mean and marked in the export.

Quick start

  1. Select wells (Select Area tab): a county, HUC-8 or HUC-10, an aquifer area, a drawn polygon, or clicked wells.
  2. Filter (Data filters tab): source, depth, period of record, aquifer, reading quality and so on.
  3. Read the chart: the composite hydrograph of the selected wells, with the number of wells behind each year as grey bars (right-hand axis).
  4. Adjust the method in the panel under the chart: method, season, minimum wells per year, record length, depth or elevation.

Nothing is drawn until wells are selected, so the page opens quickly. Well points show from zoom level 9 in, or for the selection.

Data sources

  • Water levels (live): the NM Unified Water Data API (wells and depth-to-water readings). It merges USGS-NWIS, the NM Bureau of Geology and Mineral Resources (NMBGMR), NM OSE/ISC SensorThings feeds (Albuquerque, Bernalillo County, PVACD, EBID, NMOSE-Roswell), the ISC Seven Rivers network and the Water Quality Portal. The readings are saved in your browser, refreshed with new readings on later visits and downloaded again in full every 30 days. A download that comes back empty or short never replaces the saved copy.
  • Readings from published reports: literature-readings.csv holds readings from NMBGMR open-file reports that the API does not have yet, from the "OFR water levels vs API" comparison workbook (27,838 readings at 676 wells, 31 reports). They are added to the matching wells as manual readings. A reading is skipped when that well's month already holds the same value (±0.05 ft), so as the API takes these readings in, the file adds less and less. Readings at wells not in the API are not included because most have no location.
  • Well attributes: nm-site-metadata.json: aquifer extent (a refined 2-D aquifer-boundary dataset), aquifer-mapping study area, geologic formation, USGS principal aquifer, well depth and water-quality summaries, matched by site ID.
  • Land elevation: the site elevation from the API; the USGS 3D Elevation Program (3DEP) 10 m elevation service fills wells without one and checks the others (see Depth and elevation).
  • Boundaries: counties; HUC-8 and HUC-10 watersheds from the USGS Watershed Boundary Dataset.
  • Kriged basin averages: precomputed files in kriged/ (see Kriged basin average).

Browsers block a page opened from your own computer (a file:// address) from reading files beside it. When the page is opened that way, the tool reads kriged/ and literature-readings.csv from the GitHub site instead. Everything works fully on the GitHub site.

Preparing readings

  • Reading quality: uses the API's qualifier, approval status and measurement type per reading. The options keep static levels only, drop pumping or otherwise affected readings, keep approved data only, or include or exclude continuous-logger readings.
  • Multi-agency wells: one physical well can appear under two agencies. The default (Merge) makes each cross-agency cluster one well carrying every agency's readings, listed under NMBGMR where it has one, else under the longest record. A month that two agencies report with the same monthly value (within 0.05 ft) is the same measurement stored twice and is counted once; different values in the same month are both kept. Since the composite uses one value per month, a duplicate can never count twice. Other options keep only one agency's record, or exclude or isolate multi-agency wells. The links come from the API's cross-agency clusters and NMBGMR cross-reference IDs.
  • Likely data-entry errors (Data quality): a monthly or yearly value is flagged when it lies outside both of its neighbours by at least t × 25 ft and by more than t × √n of the neighbours' size (n = readings behind the value), and by more than 1.5 times the gap between the neighbours. t = 0.6 (Lenient, default), 0.4 (Moderate) or 0.25 (Strict). Flagged values are left out; the table lists them.
  • Season: only readings in the chosen months count, before anything else is computed. A range across the new year counts as one season: Winter (Nov–Feb) 2020 is Nov–Dec 2019 plus Jan–Feb 2020. Winter levels are least affected by irrigation pumping.
  • Water years (optional): October–September, labelled by the year it ends, as in Myers (2022).
  • Period of record and minimum years filters work on each well's readings inside the chosen years.

Depth and elevation

Depth to water is feet below land surface; larger means a deeper (lower) water table. Water-level elevation = land elevation − depth to water, in feet.

  • The API site elevation is always used when it exists. Wells without one get the 3DEP land elevation at their location (10 m, bilinear), so they can still be plotted.
  • Elevation check: in elevation mode, the selected wells' site elevations are compared with 3DEP. Wells more than 300 ft apart are listed with a warning and marked in the per-well CSV. Their values are not changed. A large difference usually means a wrong location or elevation in the source record. Site elevations also mix vertical datums (NGVD29 and NAVD88, typically a few feet apart in New Mexico).

Composite hydrograph (the "Over the Top" method)

A composite hydrograph summarizes many wells' own records year by year. Nothing is interpolated between wells or shifted between years. For the selected wells:

  1. Annual value per well: the median of the well's monthly values in each year (default), or the reading-weighted yearly mean.
  2. Spike filter: an annual value is dropped when it differs from both neighbouring years by more than 65 ft per year (adjustable; 0 = off). End years need only their one neighbour.
  3. Record rules: a well needs at least 5 years with data spanning at least 8 years inside the period shown (both adjustable).
  4. Put wells on a common scale, so deep and shallow wells count equally. In each case "up" means more water.
    • Normal score (default, the Standardised Groundwater level Index idea of Bloomfield and Marchant, 2013): each well's annual values are ranked, r = 1…n, and converted with Blom's formula: z = Φ−1((r − 3/8) / (n + 1/4)), where Φ−1 is the inverse standard normal. Robust to outliers and skew.
    • Z-score: z = (x − mean) / standard deviation of the well's annual values.
    • Chain-linked change (ft): the composite starts at 0. Each year is linked to the earlier plotted year that shares the most wells, and its step is the median (or mean) change of the wells measured in both years. The result is cumulative change in feet. It keeps real units but can drift where few wells link.
    For depth to water, values are multiplied by −1 before standardizing, so a rising water table plots upward.
  5. Composite: for each year, the median (dashed) and mean (solid) across the wells with a value that year. A year with fewer wells than Min. wells per year (default 5) is left empty and its bar turns red. Years with fewer than 10 wells get hollow markers.
  6. Uncertainty band: 95% bootstrap range of the yearly median. Wells are resampled with replacement (default 200 draws, reduced automatically for very large selections; Efron and Tibshirani, 1993). It shows how much the result depends on which wells happen to be in the set, not measurement error.
  7. Wells per year (grey bars, right axis): the number of wells behind each plotted value. For chain-linked, the number of wells linking that year to its partner year.

"Normalize per well" (feet anomaly or USGS % of range, N = −(x − mean)/(max − min) × 100; Myers, 2022, eq. 1) applies to the Overview plot only.

Trends

  • Theil–Sen slope (Theil, 1950; Sen, 1968): the median of all pairwise slopes between years, in ft per year (or SD per year). Insensitive to outliers.
  • Mann–Kendall test (Mann, 1945; Kendall, 1975): the sign of every pair of years gives S; its variance is corrected for ties, and p is two-sided. Annual values are autocorrelated, so p-values are optimistic.
  • Per well: the median slope across wells with a bootstrap 95% CI, the share of wells falling, and the number with p < 0.05. For the composite: the Theil–Sen slope of the yearly median.

Groups, clusters and season compare

  • Group by splits the selection (by well depth, depth to water, geologic formation, USGS principal aquifer, aquifer extent or study area) and draws a composite for each group with the same method.
  • Hydrograph clusters (Bloomfield et al., 2015): k-means on each well's z-scores over the years with enough wells. A well joins if it covers 60% of those years. Interior gaps are interpolated for the clustering only, never for the plotted lines. k = Number of groups.
  • Season compare draws the same wells using each season's months only.

Kriged basin average (HUC-8 and HUC-10)

Selecting a HUC-8 or HUC-10 adds the basin-average water level to the chain-linked chart: an estimate of the average water-level change over the whole area, not just at the wells. It is an areal-average hydrograph, not a composite. It is precomputed with spatiotemporal regression kriging after Rawling (2021, rev. 2022, NMBGMR OFR-616):

  1. Data: the API readings (pulled 2026-11-01) plus the published-report readings; pumping and other disturbed readings dropped; one value per well per year (median); wells at the same spot merged. Two seasons: winter (Nov–Feb, as in OFR-616) and all months.
  2. Regional trend: a polynomial of the first two principal components of easting, northing and land elevation. Outliers are removed when the standardized residual exceeds 2 and Cook's distance exceeds 4/n (Cook, 1977).
  3. Space-time variogram of the residuals: sum-metric model (Gräler et al., 2016), spherical in space, exponential in time, with a joint space-time term. Nuggets have floors so the model cannot become falsely certain.
  4. Calibration: leave-one-out cross-validation; standard errors are multiplied by √(mean squared normalized error), at least 1, so about 95% of left-out readings fall inside their 95% range.
  5. Change from a base year: block contrast kriging of the change in the areal mean between each year and the HUC-8's best-measured year, over the part of the unit within 5 km of a well. The band is ±1.96 standard errors.
  6. Quality badge: good, fair or poor, from cross-validation correlation, the calibration factor and a zigzag check. Where the line jumps from year to year more than its range allows (often wells from different aquifers measured in different years), the ranges are widened by that factor and a caution shows.

The line is aligned to the composite in the first shared year. It is read from kriged/winter.json when the season lies within Nov–Feb, otherwise from kriged/all.json. Rebuild scripts are listed in kriged/README.md.

Exports

Composite CSV (year, median, mean, band, wells per year, method; optionally per-well columns and the basin average), per-well statistics CSV (years, span, Theil–Sen, Mann–Kendall p, spike years dropped, cluster, site and 3DEP elevations, elevation check), raw readings CSV, the flagged-readings table, and a PNG of the chart with the method in its title block.

Limitations

  • The well set changes from year to year. The composite reflects whichever wells were measured, so a shift in where measurements were made can look like a change in water level. Check the bars, and compare with the kriged average.
  • Mixing aquifers (for example shallow and artesian aquifers in the Roswell basin) blends different behaviour. Filter by aquifer or depth, or use hydrograph clusters.
  • Thresholds (65 ft/yr, 5 years over 8, 5 wells per year, 60% coverage) are literature starting values (Jasechko et al., 2024; Bloomfield and Marchant, 2013; Bloomfield et al., 2015), not validated for New Mexico.
  • Mann–Kendall p-values ignore autocorrelation. Kriged averages smooth and can be dominated by one well where data are sparse.
